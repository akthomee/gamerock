Top 5 Offline FPS Games for Android

Tired of lagging because of bad Wi-Fi? Sick of multiplayer games that force you to buy skins? Here are the 5 best single-player, fully offline first-person shooters you can play right now, no internet required.

7 Min Read

The mobile gaming industry has largely abandoned the single-player experience. If you look at the top charts today, it's all battle royales, pay-to-win shooters, and always-online games that crash the second your signal drops.

But sometimes, you just want to play a shooter on a plane, on the bus, or in a place with zero Wi-Fi. You want a real campaign, with a beginning, a middle, and an end—not a never-ending loop of microtransactions.

Thankfully, the classics are still alive. Here are the top 5 offline FPS games for Android that you can download and play completely offline today.

1. N.O.V.A. 3

If you played Halo on the Xbox, you will immediately feel at home with N.O.V.A. 3. It’s the best first-person shooter Gameloft ever made, featuring a full 10-level campaign that spans Earth, a frozen alien city, and deep space.

Unlike its predecessor, N.O.V.A. 3 allows you to drive vehicles, pilot a mech, and use a wide array of sci-fi weaponry. The graphics still look incredible for a mobile game, and the voice acting is top-notch. You can play the entire campaign without a single Wi-Fi connection.

2. Modern Combat 3: Fallen Nation

Before Gameloft turned Modern Combat into a free-to-play mobile clone of Call of Duty, they gave us Modern Combat 3. It is widely considered the peak of the series.

You play as various soldiers across 13 missions, fighting in locations from Los Angeles to Pakistan. It has a gripping story, intense scripted set-pieces (like manning a door gun on a helicopter), and a robust single-player campaign. It is a heavy game (almost 1.5 GB), but once it's installed, it's 100% offline.

Get the game: We currently don't have Modern Combat 3 hosted, but you can check out N.O.V.A. 2 as an alternative classic shooter.

3. Dead Trigger 2

If you prefer zombies over aliens and soldiers, Dead Trigger 2 is your game. It’s a survival FPS where you fight off hordes of undead across a global map.

While it does have optional online features, you can play the entire campaign offline. The game features a massive arsenal of weapons, upgradeable gear, and surprisingly good gore physics. If you want a game you can play in short bursts without needing an internet connection, this is it.

4. N.O.V.A. 2 HD

We couldn't make this list without including the classic. N.O.V.A. 2 is the sequel to the original game that put Gameloft on the map.

It features 12 chapters taking you through hostile, amazing environments. The gameplay is largely the same as the first, but with new weapons and a few vehicle segments. It’s a bit older than N.O.V.A. 3, so the graphics are a little dated, but the campaign is fantastic.

Get the game: Download N.O.V.A. 2 HD from our directory. Check our step-by-step install guide to get it working on Android 12+.

5. Shadowgun Legends (Offline Campaign)

Developed by MADFINGER Games, Shadowgun Legends is often called the "Destiny of mobile." It's a massive RPG-shooter with hundreds of guns and armor sets.

While the game is heavily focused on multiplayer hubs, the actual campaign missions can be played entirely offline. If you want a shooter that feels like a console RPG, with deep customization and boss fights, this is the best free option available.

Why Play Offline?

Offline games have a special charm. They don't have energy timers. They don't block you from progressing unless you pay $4.99. They don't lag because the server is halfway across the world.

If you have a long flight, a boring commute, or just a terrible data plan, loading up an offline FPS is the best way to kill time. The games listed here are testaments to the golden era of mobile gaming—before microtransactions ruined the fun.

How to Install These Games Today

Because most of these games were removed from the Google Play Store years ago, you can't just search for them and hit "Install." You have to download the APK and (sometimes) the OBB data file from a trusted archive.

Furthermore, because Android 12+ dropped support for 32-bit apps, most of these games will refuse to install directly on your modern phone. You will need to use a virtual machine like VPhoneOS to run them.
